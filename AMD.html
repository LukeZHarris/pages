NUB-1762 Programme Brief · 2026 Synthesys · Noetica Voice Platform

The Audible
Boundary.

A unified design for answer-machine detection and call screening, built on a single insight: the network already gives us a free second of listening before the compliance clock starts.

Modern phones and carriers now answer calls on the recipient's behalf — Apple Live Voicemail, Google Call Screen, Bixby Text Call, network-level greetings. Our predictive dialler reads them as voicemail and disconnects. This programme rebuilds the front of the call so we can hear what's actually happening, navigate the gatekeeper honestly, and only hand an agent to a real human.

§ 01 · Premise

The dialler
has stopped
hearing right.

Three things changed at once, and they all changed in the same direction.

Our predictive dialler still classifies the first few seconds of every connected call into one of two boxes: human or answering machine. That model worked for a long time. It no longer matches what's on the line.

The phone in the recipient's pocket has started speaking on their behalf. Apple Live Voicemail reads our greeting in real time and lets the recipient pick up mid-message. Google's Call Screen and Samsung Bixby Text Call ask us — politely, in a synthetic voice — to state our name and reason. Carriers and reputation networks layer further screening before the device even rings. To our AMD, all of this looks like voicemail. We disconnect, mark the call answered-machine, and move on.

The cost is invisible but accumulating: missed conversations with people who would have taken the call, agents bridged into synthetic gatekeepers when something does get through, and an AMD model that drifts further from reality every quarter as more screening systems ship by default. The fix is not to make AMD guess harder. The fix is to teach the front of the call to listen to what kind of thing is actually on the other end, and to navigate it honestly.

§ 02 · The insight

SIP 200 OK
is the line
worth drawing.

The compliance clock and the audio clock are not the same clock.

Every outbound call has a moment when the network formally says 200 OK — the call is answered. Ofcom's two-second abandoned-call rule, and our compliance posture generally, begins ticking at that point. But here's the thing: audio arrives earlier. SIP allows "early media" — the ringback, the carrier voicemail greeting, even the first words of a screening prompt — to stream before the 200 OK comes in.

That gap is structural and free. We're not allowed to keep a human waiting, but we are allowed to listen to a recording. We can spend that window matching greeting audio against fingerprints of known carrier voicemail, classifying machine-shaped openings, and making decisions before the compliance clock is even running.

So the architecture splits cleanly on the SIP 200 OK boundary — and the whole programme arranges itself around that split.

The mental model: we already have a state machine called LPD — Live Person Detection. Its primitives (per-state timers, "hello" probes, evidence accumulation, beep-as-interrupter, terminal cleanup) are mature and proven. → We aren't replacing it. We're extending it forwards into early media, and outwards to handle screening as a first-class outcome rather than a failed human detection.
§ 03 · Three lenses

One pipeline,
three lenses.

What sounds like three programmes is one architecture looked at from three angles.

A natural temptation is to scope this as three projects: improve AMD, build screening, and protect LPD. That framing creates parallel teams, duplicated audio paths, and three separate compliance arguments. We rejected it.

Instead, the design is one pipeline of signal capture, decision-making, and routing — viewed through three lenses depending on where in the call you stand and what kind of endpoint you're facing.

01

Smarter AMD, earlier

Listen in early media. Fingerprint-match known network and carrier voicemail greetings. Fall back to a small acoustic classifier when there's no match. Decide before the call is formally answered.

PRE-200
02

Honest screening navigation

Treat the screening system as a first-class endpoint, not a failed human. Announce ourselves truthfully, answer narrow prompts (name, reason, press 1), and bridge the agent only on confirmed human acceptance.

POST-200
03

LPD reused, not rebuilt

The Live Person Detection primitives we already trust — timers, probes, evidence weights, beep handling — drop in on both sides of the boundary. The state machine grows; the foundation stays.

§ 04 · Architecture

The gatekeeper
state machine.

Four layers, deterministic on the live path, AI-rich only in offline tuning.

Live calls demand determinism. We can't bet a regulated outbound platform on whichever way a language model swings today. The architecture isolates speech understanding to a narrow phrase-spotter — recognise a small set of screening prompts and outcomes — and keeps richer AI in offline analysis where mistakes are cheap and corrective.

i

Signal layer

Continuous line-level measurement: talk-over, speech onset and duration, silence windows, beep detection, DTMF, speaker change, hangup/answer timing.

Reuses existing AMD + LPD
VAD · beep detector
ii

Prompt-understanding layer

Narrow phrase-spotter for a fixed set of screening prompt and outcome classes. Compact labels, not transcripts. Language profile chosen from destination number and campaign.

Bake-off Azure Speech · ElevenLabs Scribe Realtime · Vosk grammar
iii

Response policy

What to do given the latest classification: play approved identity response, play reason-for-call, send DTMF, stay silent and wait, retry shorter, hand to voicemail policy, or bridge the agent.

Constraint truthful, short, compliance-approved
iv

State machine

The gatekeeper. Owns where we are in the flow, enforces per-state timers and overall timeouts, and pulls the trigger on agent bridge only on a confirmed callee_accepting outcome.

Built from LPD primitives, extended

§ 06 · Sequencing

Observe first.
Ship live last.

The work looks parallel. The safe critical path is sequential.

A live outbound dialler is the wrong place to try things. We accumulate evidence first, label it, choose an ASR vendor on labelled audio rather than vendor claims, shadow the state machine in observe-only mode, and only then expose any of it to live routing.

At every stage there's something to learn and a measurable gate to pass — there are no leaps of faith embedded in the live path.

01

Capture

Land the NVP changes in observe-only mode. PRE-200 / POST-200 recording, VAD timing fix, and per-call AMD decision logging.

02

Label

Bring up the AMD QA tool. Build ground truth on real calls before tuning anything.

03

Bake-off

Score Azure Speech, ElevenLabs Scribe Realtime, and Vosk grammar mode against the labelled corpus. Latency, accuracy, cost.

04

Shadow

Run the gatekeeper state machine alongside live calls. Measure prompt coverage, unknown rates, time-to-acceptance, Live Voicemail prevalence.

05

Navigate

Limited live navigation only after compliance, corpus governance, and dialler-pacing gates are closed. Deterministic, measurable, reversible.

§ 07 · Open questions

What this
design depends on,
and doesn't own.

Seven questions the engineering can't answer alone. They are listed so they're not forgotten.

Every honest brief has a section like this. These are decisions outside the engineering scope — compliance, IP, ownership — that we'd like landed in parallel so they're not the late discovery that blocks rollout.

01 Legal · Compliance

When does the Ofcom abandoned-call clock start for a screened call?

If it starts at SIP 200, every screened call is abandoned by definition. If it starts at confirmed human acceptance, the design works. We need a written position.

Next: compliance opinion before Phase 2. Phase 1 (shadow) can proceed without it.
02 Carrier relations · Ops

Does anyone own STIR/SHAKEN attestation and CLI reputation?

If we reduce the triggering rate of screening, every engineering investment here becomes proportionally less urgent. This is likely the highest ROI per pound on the table.

Next: confirm ownership or commission as a parallel workstream.
03 IP · External counsel

Freedom-to-operate review for fingerprinting.

Constellation hashing (F1), MFCC+LSH (F2), and small embeddings (F3) each carry different patent exposure. Prototyping is fine; shipping needs an FTO conclusion.

Next: commission FTO before any production deployment.
04 QA tool owner

Who owns the ASR bake-off and the labelled corpus?

Phase 1 of the screening design assumes a working phrase-spotter on narrow-band telephony audio. Without an owner, the bake-off slips and Phase 2 stalls.

Next: QA-tool owner takes the bake-off. Corpus accumulates from Phase 1 recordings.
05 Product

Does Apple Live Voicemail deserve distinct handling?

The recipient reads our message in real time and may pick up mid-greeting. That's neither voicemail nor screening. Could affect phrasing, bridging, and compliance.

Next: wait for prevalence data from shadow mode before designing a separate path.
06 Data protection

Recording corpus governance.

The PRE/POST-200 recordings will be reused for QA, ASR bake-off, fingerprint evaluation, and training. Retention, access, redaction, vendor-sharing — agree before capture is broad.

Next: lightweight corpus policy before broad capture.
07 Dialler · Workforce routing

Agent reservation under delayed handover.

A screened call may need 5–15 seconds before callee_accepting. Reserve the agent early and occupancy suffers; reserve late and we risk silence on pickup. Likely needs its own pacing model.

Next: log the SIP-200-to-acceptance distribution in Phase 1, set policy in Phase 2.
+ Cross-cutting

What we're explicitly not doing.

No open-ended conversational AI on the live path. No full transcription as a dependency for v1. No special case per obscure prompt. No heavy fingerprinting investment before we understand prompt distribution.

These boundaries are load-bearing. They keep the live path deterministic.
§ 08 · Success

What "working"
looks like.

Each phase has a measurable outcome before the next begins.

The QA tool itself becomes the scoring system for the rest of the programme. Every claim — "our AMD got better", "the phrase-spotter is good enough", "the screening flow bridges to a real human" — becomes a number against labelled ground truth.

That discipline is the difference between a model that drifts again in two years and a platform that can defend its classification choices to compliance, customers, and ourselves.

After Phase 1 ~1s

Decision latency from connect for PRE-200 fingerprint matches. AMD QA tool live; corpus accumulating; per-call reason summary in the database.

After Phase 2 3 vendors

ASR bake-off complete on a labelled telephony corpus. Phrase-spotter chosen on intent accuracy, latency, and cost — not vendor claims.

After Phase 3 0%

Agents bridged to gatekeepers or voicemail by mistake on the limited-live cohort. callee_accepting only, deterministic and reversible.
